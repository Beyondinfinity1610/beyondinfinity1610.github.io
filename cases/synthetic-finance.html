The 30-second version, in words

As an ML research intern at kyfr.ai I built a persona-driven simulator of daily financial transactions, with scripted life events, balance integrity and byte-reproducible seeding, and a detection layer behind one API. On synthetic data it reaches F1 above 0.75 on 3 of 4 rare events, on synthetic data, read against four controls. There is no real customer data here, and nothing is deployed.

01QUESTION

Can synthetic financial histories carry rare life events realistically enough to be detected, and how would I know the detector is finding signal and not a shortcut?

02SIGNAL

The detail

Real financial data is regulated, and rare events such as job loss barely appear in any real book. The alternative is to generate histories from personas, script life events into them, keep the accounting consistent so balances reconcile, and make every run reproducible from a seed.

03PROBLEM· anticipated, not experienced

If you build the benchmark, you can accidentally make the benchmark easy.

Before the F1 gets to say anything, a detector that always answers 'yes' gets a turn.
Margin

Before the F1 gets to say anything, a detector that always answers 'yes' gets a turn.

The detail

An F1 on self-generated data is easy to game. The more often events are simulated, the higher the trivial floor rises, and a single feature can do most of the work. A raw score can overstate how hard the problem was. This risk was anticipated, not experienced, which is why this stage is labelled PROBLEM and not FAILURE.

04INVESTIGATION

The detail

Four detector families sit behind one API: a statistical change-point detector, a rule-based detector, a gradient-boosted model and an optional GRU sequence model. Each is judged against four controls, drawn here as bands behind the score.

A detector's ranking transfers across populations. Its cutoff depends on the base rate it was trained at.

05CHANGE

Report the score relative to the controls, not on its own.

The detail

The design emphasis moved from reporting an F1 to auditing it: every score is read inside the gap between what a trivial method scores and what a shortcut scores.

The detail

Three of the four scripted event types cleared the bar: job loss, medical emergency and relocation. The result is about the generator: it shows that the data carries recoverable event signal. It is not a performance claim about real customers.

Reproducibility is part of the result: the same seed produces the same dataset, byte for byte on non-UUID columns.

07WHAT IT MEANS

A benchmark you built yourself needs the most suspicious evaluation.

What this shows

  • The simulated data carries event signal that standard detector families can recover.
  • That signal was judged against an always-positive floor, a single-feature ceiling, a permutation control and an adversarial harness.
  • Scores depend on simulated event frequency, which is why the controls exist.

What this does not show

  • Performance on real customers. Everything here is synthetic.
  • Deployment, customers or business impact. None is claimed.
  • External or real-data validation.
  • A new detection method. The detectors are standard families.
  • Detail. The code, parameters and personas belong to the company and cannot be shared.
