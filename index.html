Machine learning research — Chennai, India

Signals that refuse to sit still.

I build models for measurements that drift — brain, heart, muscle, motion. The interesting failure is never the architecture. It is that the sensor was never telling the truth in the first place, and almost nothing in the literature is built for that.

Selected work

Most published accuracy does not survive the second recording site.

A model learns the drift instead of the event. It learns the patient instead of the condition. It calls a jaw clench a seizure because one sensor genuinely cannot tell the difference. None of these show up in a validation score computed the usual way — which is precisely why so much of this field fails to replicate.

My work sits on that fault line. Decomposition before learning, so the network is handed separated structure rather than a sum. Architectures penalised for encoding identity. Evaluation on the full continuous stream, split by subject, scored the way a practitioner would score it.

It makes every number smaller and every result slower to arrive. It is the only version of the work worth doing.

Selected work

Five that matter.

01

Wearable seizure detection

Lead researcher SeizeIT2 · 125 patients · 2,912 hours Manuscript in preparation

Detecting focal seizures from a two-channel sensor worn behind the ear, on people who are walking, eating and sleeping while wearing it. Seizures occupy well under one percent of the recording, and the signal that indicates one is frequently indistinguishable from ordinary movement.

My contribution is as much diagnostic as architectural: establishing what this class of model is actually doing, which of its components genuinely carry information, and where the ceiling of the modality itself lies. Evaluated end to end on the continuous stream against the dataset's published baselines.

Method and results are held back pending review. I am glad to walk through them in conversation.

02

Band-wise graph networks for EEG

Lead researcher 121 subjects In preparation

Treating the brain as a network rather than a stack of channels: phase-based connectivity, graph attention over the resulting topology, and an adversarial branch whose entire job is to stop the encoder recognising the person it is looking at.

The premise is that different frequency bands carry different — sometimes opposing — structure, and collapsing them early destroys the thing you came to measure.

03

Self-healing sensor architecture

Patent 202541081235 Published Embedded ML

A fault-detection and recovery layer that sits beneath the application: the fault is identified and correction triggered before the next control-loop tick, so downstream logic never sees the bad reading. On-device learning performs the recalibration, which removes the technician from deployments that never had one.

Different domain, same conviction as the research — assume the instrument is wrong and design for it.

04

Synthetic population engine

KYFR Applied ML Shipped

A generator for privacy-safe synthetic populations and multi-year, event-labelled financial histories — built for training systems where the real data is regulated and the situations that matter are, by definition, rare.

The discipline is the interesting part. The same seed reproduces the dataset byte for byte, and the output has to survive an adversarial audit and a change-point detector recovering the injected events before it is allowed to certify as usable at all. A generator you cannot falsify is not a generator.

05

Quantum-Hilbert signal encoding

Lead research intern CADS, VIT Under review

An encoding framework built from first principles, targeting the actual mathematical structure of a physiological signal — phase-coherent, amplitude-modulated, multi-oscillatory — rather than a picture of its spectrogram.

It preserves the instantaneous phase and envelope information that a magnitude transform discards, and carries them into a variational quantum latent space.

Approach

Four things I will not compromise on.

i

Audit the data path first.

Before the architecture, before the loss, before anything: confirm the model is receiving what you believe it is receiving. The largest single improvement I have ever made to a system came from reading my own data loader properly. It is unglamorous and it keeps working.

ii

Verify what you assume is learning.

A component can sit in a network for months, appear in every diagram, and contribute nothing — with no sign of it in the loss curve. If a part is supposed to be training, open the checkpoint and prove that it did.

iii

Score it the way a practitioner would.

On the full continuous stream, never a balanced subset. Split by subject, always — a random split leaks the person across the boundary and inflates everything downstream. And treat a suspiciously good number as a bug until proven otherwise.

iv

Keep the negative results.

The experiments that made things worse are documented as carefully as the ones that worked, and so are my own corrections. Negative results are the only part of a research record that cannot be produced by luck.

About

I came to this through engines.

What first interested me was that a modern engine controller makes hundreds of decisions a second from sensors that are all, quietly, wrong. A knock sensor drifts as it heats. An oxygen sensor ages and its response curve moves. Nothing is broken — the instruments are measuring a moving world through hardware that is itself moving. And the controller still has to decide, right now, whether that vibration is detonation or a bad road.

An electrode is the same object. Impedance drifts with sweat, anatomy differs between people, the sensor is re-seated at a slightly different angle every morning — and the model still has to decide whether that burst of rhythm is a seizure or someone chewing. I did not move from cars to neuroscience. I found the same problem wearing different clothes, with higher stakes and worse data.

I am twenty-one, in my final year of computer science at VIT Chennai, and I do most of this outside coursework because it is what I would be doing regardless.
