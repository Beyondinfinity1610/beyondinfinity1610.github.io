01 — The problem

Three ways a biosignal model quietly lies to you.

None of these show up in a validation score computed the usual way. All three are why most published biosignal accuracy does not survive contact with a second recording site. Everything below is built to attack one of them.

01 / Non-stationarity

The signal changes while you are modelling it.

EEG is not a stationary process. Its spectral content, amplitude and phase structure drift over minutes, and the interesting events are exactly the moments where that drift breaks. A model trained on windowed averages learns the drift and misses the break. The fix is decomposition before learning — variational mode decomposition, Hilbert envelopes, per-branch filtering — so the network receives separated oscillations instead of their sum.

02 / Domain shift

Subject A does not generalise to Subject B.

Electrode impedance, skull thickness, cortical folding and cognitive baseline all differ. A network with enough capacity will identify the patient and infer the label from that — scoring near-perfectly in cross-validation and collapsing on anyone new. The answer is not more data. It is an architecture that is punished for encoding subject identity at all.

03 / Artifact vs. event

A jaw clench and a seizure look the same to one sensor.

On behind-the-ear wearable EEG, chewing, speaking and walking produce high-amplitude rhythmic activity that is spectrally indistinguishable from an ictal discharge. No amount of filtering separates them, because the difference is not in the EEG. It is in whether the heart, the deltoid and the accelerometer agree — a fusion problem, not a denoising one.

Step 01 — The setting

A sensor behind the ear, and a person living their life.

SeizeIT2: 125 patients wearing a behind-the-ear Sensor Dot in hospital. Two EEG channels instead of a 21-electrode cap. The patient walks, eats, talks and sleeps. Seizures occupy well under one percent of the recording.

Published scalp-EEG detectors reach roughly 43% event-level F1 in this setting. Wearable detectors do considerably worse — not because the models are weak, but because two channels of ambulatory EEG genuinely cannot tell a seizure from a jaw clench.

Step 02 — Five tokenizers

Every modality gets the encoder its physics deserves.

Not one shared backbone. The low-frequency EEG branch is a CNN-GRU tracking seizure evolution. The high-frequency branch runs Teager energy and autocorrelation for automatisms. The deltoid EMG reduces to energy and rhythmicity. The ECG is a masked autoencoder over a Takens phase-space embedding, trained self-supervised so it knows a normal heart before it is ever shown an abnormal one. The accelerometer uses ROCKET — random, deterministic, and never allowed to train.

ChronoNet 0.5–15 Hz TKEO 20–40 Hz Takens masked AE ROCKET · frozen
Step 03 — The trust gate

The model decides, second by second, whether to believe its own EEG.

A small network reads accelerometer energy, EMG energy and the rhythmicity of both, and emits one scalar between zero and one that scales the entire EEG pathway. It learns a distinction that matters clinically: chaotic high-motion means artifact, so close the gate — but rhythmic motion means a bilateral tonic-clonic seizure, so hold it open.

Removing this module costs 41% more false alarms. Replacing the scalar with a 128-dimensional vector gate destroys the model entirely. The narrow bottleneck is the point.

DynMM · scalar g ∈ [0,1] motion-type aware soft targets 0.9 / 0.3
Step 04 — Cross-modal fusion

Autonomic signs arrive before cortical ones. The transformer learns the lag.

Heart-rate change frequently precedes visible EEG onset, and the offset differs by patient. Rather than hand-align the streams, directional cross-attention lets each modality query the others and discover the alignment itself.

The upgrade that mattered was a second layer of inter-modal self-attention after the cross-modal pass — letting ECG and EMG interrogate each other. That single change suppressed over 90% of chewing and speaking false alarms and pushed event-level F1 to 53.01%, ten points clear of the scalp-EEG ceiling.

Deep MulT directional cross-attention +10.0 pts vs. ceiling
Step 05 — Two heads, and an argument

What it predicts, and why you can check it.

Level one is seizure versus background. Level two is whether the seizure generalised to a bilateral tonic-clonic event — gated so it contributes no gradient unless a real seizure is present.

Every forward pass also returns the gate value, the cross-attention relevance map and the recurrent hidden states. Explainability is a property of the architecture rather than a post-hoc wrapper: you can watch the gate close on a corrupted channel in real time, which is the form of the "black box" objection that actually needs answering in a clinical setting.

Step 06 — The curriculum

Five phases, because training it all at once does not work.

Phase zero pre-trains the cardiac autoencoder self-supervised. Phase one warms up EEG alone with fusion bypassed. Phase two freezes the encoders and trains only the gate, the transformer and the heads. Phase three unfreezes end to end — the moment where everything previously learned gets destroyed, unless the learning rate is deliberately re-warmed at the boundary. Phase four is a low-rate polish.

The model is selected on best AUPRC across all phases, never reset per phase. At sub-one-percent prevalence, AUPRC is the only selection metric that is not mostly measuring the background class.

04 — Second research line

The brain is a graph. So build one per band.

Resting-state EEG classification for ADHD across 121 subjects. The interesting failure here is not accuracy — it is that a network with enough capacity will memorise the subject rather than the condition, and no amount of cross-validation will tell you that it did.

Why five graphs and not one.

Connectivity is computed with the weighted Phase Lag Index rather than correlation. wPLI is phase-based and therefore immune to volume conduction — the artifact where one source appears at several electrodes at once and manufactures connectivity that does not exist. It is the single largest reason EEG connectivity findings fail to replicate.

Delta and gamma carry opposite discriminative patterns in ADHD. Fuse their adjacency matrices early and the two cancel. So each band gets its own Graph Attention Network, message passing independently over its own topology, and the five embeddings are combined only at the end through learned attention. Switch bands below — the change in structure is real, not decorative.

Gradient reversal — the anti-memorisation layer

A second head is attached to the shared encoder and asked to identify which subject the window came from. Between them sits a layer that passes activations forward unchanged and multiplies the gradient by a negative constant on the way back. The subject classifier gets better at its job; the encoder is actively pushed to make that job impossible. What survives is the part of the representation that is not a fingerprint.

An encoding framework built from scratch: Hilbert transforms extract instantaneous phase and amplitude envelopes — the information an FFT discards the moment it takes a magnitude — and variational quantum circuits embed those envelopes into a quantum latent space. 06 — How I work

Four commitments, and what they cost.

Every one of these makes the work slower and the headline number smaller. That is the trade, and it is the right one — because the alternative is a result that only exists inside its own notebook.

01

Decompose before you learn.

I do not hand a network the raw sum of a dozen overlapping oscillations and hope attention sorts it out. Variational mode decomposition, Hilbert envelopes, per-branch filtering, Teager energy — the separation happens before a single weight is trained. It costs preprocessing time, and it is why the models hold up on data that was not collected by the lab that trained them.

02

Choose the metric a practitioner would choose.

At sub-one-percent prevalence, accuracy is noise and ROC-AUC is close to it. I select on AUPRC, report event-level F1 under clinical Any-Overlap scoring with proper collars, and quote false alarms per 24 hours — because that is the number that decides whether a device is worn or left in a drawer.

03

Split by subject, always.

Random window-level splits leak the patient across the boundary and inflate every score in the paper. Splits are patient-level and seeded; leave-one-subject-out where the dataset allows it. If a result depends on which windows landed where, it is not a result.

04

Publish the graveyard.

A third of the ablation campaign made the model worse, and those runs are documented as carefully as the wins — including the vector gate that collapsed to zero F1 and the two attempts to stack winning components that both backfired. Negative results are the only part of a campaign that cannot be produced by luck.
