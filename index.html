Selected work

The conviction

Every instrument is lying.

I came to signal processing sideways, through cars. What held my attention was that an engine controller makes hundreds of decisions a second from sensors that are all, quietly, wrong. A knock sensor drifts as it heats. An oxygen sensor ages and its response curve moves. A wheel-speed reading is honest until the tyre pressure changes. Nothing is broken. The instruments are measuring a moving world through hardware that is itself moving — and the controller still has to decide, right now, whether that vibration is detonation or a bad road surface.

An electrode is the same object. Impedance drifts with sweat. Skull thickness and cortical folding differ between people. The sensor is re-seated at a slightly different angle every morning. And the model still has to decide whether a burst of rhythm is a seizure or someone chewing.

I did not move from cars to neuroscience. I found the same problem wearing different clothes, with higher stakes and worse data. Everything below is one idea applied four ways: assume the instrument is lying, and design for it.

01

Wearable focal-seizure detection

Lead researcher SeizeIT2 · 125 patients · 2,912 recording-hours Manuscript in preparation

Detecting focal seizures from two EEG channels worn behind the ear, on people who walk, eat, talk and sleep while wearing the device. Seizures occupy well under one percent of the recording. Chewing, speaking and walking produce high-amplitude rhythmic activity that is, spectrally, not distinguishable from an ictal discharge. The clinically useful bar is under two false alarms a day, and published human expert readers reach roughly 59% sensitivity on this modality — which is what the ceiling actually looks like.

My contribution is as much diagnostic as architectural: establishing what this class of model is genuinely doing, which of its components carry information and which are decoration, and where the limit of the modality itself sits. Evaluated end to end on the continuous stream, split by patient, against the dataset's own published baselines rather than a kinder split of my own.

02

Band-wise graph attention networks for EEG

Lead researcher 121 subjects · 19 channels · leave-one-subject-out Manuscript in preparation

Treating the brain as a network rather than a stack of channels: phase-based connectivity, chosen because it is immune to volume conduction, and learning over the resulting topology rather than over the raw channels.

Before building any of it I established, under strict multiple-comparison control, whether the discriminative signal was present at all. It was. The bottleneck in this problem is not absent signal — it is that every new subject arrives with a different baseline, so a model that scores well within a cohort can be recognising people rather than the condition. That is the part worth solving, and it is what the method is aimed at.

03

Self-healing sensor architecture

Patent 202541081235 Published Embedded ML

A fault detection and recovery layer that sits beneath the application. The fault is identified and correction triggered within fifty milliseconds — before the next control-loop tick in most embedded systems — so the logic above it never observes the bad reading. On-device learning performs the recalibration, which removes the technician from continuous-monitoring deployments that never had one to begin with.

Different domain, same conviction as the research: the instrument is wrong, so build the correction into the floor rather than the model.

04

Synthetic population and life-event engine

Applied ML · industry Shipped FastAPI · Docker

A generator producing privacy-safe synthetic populations with multi-year, event-labelled financial histories — for training and testing systems where the real data is regulated and the situations that matter are, by definition, rare.

The discipline is the interesting part. The same seed reproduces the dataset byte for byte. Generation is split into three separable layers, and a cohort can be grown forward, backward or sideways while carrying state. Most importantly, the output is not certified as usable until a change-point detector recovers the injected life events above a hard threshold — the detector is not the product, it is the proof that the data is learnable and not naively separable. A standing adversarial harness must pass clean before any change counts as done.

The documentation states its own limits: a detector's ranking transfers between populations, but its threshold does not, because the cutoff is an absolute number on a probability scale set by the base rate it was fitted at.

05

Quantum-Hilbert encoding for EEG

Lead research intern · CADS, VIT With co-authors at VIT and Yonsei University Under review

EEG is non-stationary and its phase is fundamental, yet most quantum machine learning on biosignals encodes real-valued features and throws the analytic structure away. This work builds a sign-preserving complex encoding that carries instantaneous amplitude and phase intact into quantum state space, alongside a study of the encoding design space itself.

The paper deliberately claims representational fidelity rather than quantum advantage, and names the hardware limits that would make the stronger claim dishonest.

How I learned to stop

One dot per run in a systematic sweep. Most of them settle low — that is what a sweep looks like when you keep the failures. The line is the route through the few that survived.

The sweep, arriving The route through what was kept The ceiling, which did not move

Unlabelled, on purpose

When a model stops improving, the useful question is not which knob to turn next. It is whether the gap that remains is addressable at all. So I sweep the levers systematically — scale, fusion, ensembling, post-processing, features, band selection — and record what each one actually did, including and especially the ones that made it worse.

Then I go and find what a trained human achieves on the same signal, because that is what tells you whether you are looking at a modelling problem or a hardware one. In this case it was hardware, and saying so plainly is worth more than another point of performance would have been.

No axes and no values here — those are the paper. The shape is the point.

How I work

Diagnostic before architectural.

The best result I have produced did not come from a new module. It came from stopping, and finding out what the system was actually doing.

  1. i

    Audit what the model is receiving.

    Before the architecture, before the loss, before anything: confirm that the tensor arriving at the network is the tensor you believe you are sending it. The largest single improvement I have ever made to a system came from that check rather than from anything I added. It is unglamorous and it keeps working.

  2. ii

    Verify that what you assume is learning, learned.

    A component can sit in a network for months, appear in every diagram you draw, and contribute nothing — with no trace of it in the loss curve. If a part is supposed to be training, open the checkpoint and prove that it did. Describing a system is not the same as knowing it.

  3. iii

    Score it the way a practitioner would.

    On the full continuous stream, never a balanced subset. Split by subject, always — a random split leaks the person across the boundary and inflates everything downstream of it. Read the evaluation code with the same suspicion as the model code, and treat a suspiciously good number as a bug until it survives being attacked.

  4. iv

    Reach for the physics before the parameters.

    Phase-based connectivity because it is immune to volume conduction. An analytic-signal representation because a magnitude transform throws the phase away and the phase is where the information is. Every component should have a physical justification for existing; where one does not exist, I remove the component rather than tune it.

  5. v

    Map the ceiling instead of assuming it.

    When a model stops improving, the useful question is whether the remaining gap is addressable at all. So: sweep the levers systematically — scale, fusion, ensembling, post-processing, features, band selection — record what each one actually did, then go and find what a trained human achieves on the same signal. Knowing when to stop is harder than continuing, and it is worth more.

  6. vi

    Keep the negative results, including your own.

    The experiments that made things worse are documented as carefully as the ones that worked. When I found that an evaluation of mine was invalid, I rebuilt it from the raw recordings and corrected the claims I had already made in public. Negative results are the only part of a research record that cannot be produced by luck.
