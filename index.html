I · the conviction

Every instrument is lying.

I came to signal processing sideways, through cars. What held my attention was that an engine control unit makes hundreds of decisions a second from sensors that are all, quietly, wrong.

A knock sensor drifts as it heats. An oxygen sensor ages and its response curve moves. A wheel-speed reading is honest until tyre pressure changes. Nothing is broken — the instruments are measuring a moving world through hardware that is itself moving. And the ECU still has to decide, right now, whether a given vibration is detonation or a bad road surface.

An electrode is the same object. Impedance drifts with sweat. Skull thickness and cortical folding differ between people. The sensor is re-seated at a slightly different angle every morning. And the model still has to decide whether a burst of rhythmic activity is a seizure or the patient chewing.

I did not move from cars to neuroscience. I found the same problem wearing different clothes, with higher stakes and worse data.

  • in hardwarethe sensor corrects itself before the control loop ever sees the fault
  • in wearable EEGa second physiology tells you when to distrust the first
  • in graph modelsthe network is penalised for recognising the person
  • in synthetic datanothing certifies until a detector recovers what was injected

01

Wearable focal-seizure detection

Lead researcher · manuscript in preparation

Two channels of EEG from behind the ear, on people who walk, eat, talk and sleep while wearing the device.

Chewing, speaking and walking all produce high-amplitude rhythmic activity that is spectrally almost indistinguishable from an ictal discharge. The clinically useful bar is fewer than two false alarms per day — one bad recording can spend that budget before lunch. For scale on how hard the signal is: published human expert readers reach roughly 59% sensitivity reading this modality.

the part I would want asked about

It plateaued. I stopped tuning and audited myself.

Twenty-eight tuning experiments in, the numbers stopped moving. The temptation at that point is another module.

Instead I went looking for what the system was actually doing, and found that the problem was never architectural. It was my own instrumentation — what the model was actually receiving, what I had assumed was training, and how I was scoring the result. One of those invalidated numbers I had already published, including numbers that were on this website.

So I rebuilt the evaluation from the raw recordings, verified the model's input was byte-identical to training, re-ran everything, and corrected my own public claims. The earlier figures were artifacts. They are gone.

The mechanisms belong to the manuscript. The habit does not, and the habit is the point: I am willing to conclude that the problem is my own instrumentation.

02

Band-wise graph attention for EEG

Lead researcher · manuscript in preparation · ADHD classification

Before building anything I wanted to know whether the signal existed at all. So the first result was a statistical one: phase-based connectivity across every electrode pair and every band, under strict multiple-comparison control.

It said the data is separable — and that the bottleneck is domain shift, because unseen subjects carry different absolute connectivity baselines. That finding then became an architectural prior rather than a paragraph in the introduction, and it is why the network is explicitly penalised for being able to recognise which person it is looking at.

Cohort
121 subjects · 19-channel EEG at 128 Hz
Validation
Grouped folds — no subject on both sides of a split
Approach
Phase-based connectivity, band-separated graph attention, domain-adversarial training
Status
In preparation · quality-control criteria fixed before any model was trained

Hardware-level self-healing sensing

Published patent · 202541081235

A fault-detection and recovery layer that sits below the application. A bad sensor reading is identified and corrected within 50 milliseconds — before the next control-loop tick in most embedded systems — so the logic above never observes it at all.

Embedded machine learning does the recalibration, which removes the human from the loop in continuous monitoring deployments that never had one to begin with. Same conviction as everything else here, implemented in silicon rather than in a loss function.

04

KYFR — synthetic population engine

Professional engagement · shipped

A generator that produces privacy-safe synthetic financial populations with multi-year, event-labelled transaction histories — for training and testing products where real customer data is regulated and the interesting life events are rare.

What makes it a system rather than a data faker is the discipline around it. The same seed reproduces the same dataset byte for byte. Generation is three separable layers: population, timeline, transactions. An adversarial quality harness must pass with zero critical findings before any change counts as finished.

The idea I am most attached to is the certification gate: a change-point detector has to recover the injected life events above a hard bar before the data is allowed to certify. The detector is not the product — its job is to prove the data is learnable, and not naively separable.

Surface
FastAPI service, async job queue, OpenAPI export, HTML and PDF reporting, one-command Docker
Incremental modes
Extend, append, backfill, inject, resample — a cohort can grow forward, backward or sideways while carrying state
Documented limit
A detector's ranking transfers between populations; its threshold does not, because the cutoff is an absolute number on a probability scale set by the base rate it was fitted at
05

Quantum-Hilbert encoding for EEG

Lead research intern, CADS · under review · with co-authors at VIT and Yonsei

EEG is non-stationary and its phase is fundamental, yet most quantum machine learning on biosignals encodes real-valued features and throws the analytic structure away before the circuit ever sees it. The work introduces a sign-preserving complex encoding that aligns amplitude and phase directly with quantum superposition states, alongside a study of five encoding pathways.

The framing matters more than the result: the paper claims representational correctness, not quantum advantage, and names its own obstacles — hardware limits, barren plateaus, premature classicalisation of measurements. I would rather publish a modest claim that survives.

III · how I work

Diagnostic before architectural.

Six habits, in the order I actually apply them.

  1. i

    Audit what the model is receiving.

    Before anything else, confirm the tensor reaching the network is the one you think you wrote. The largest single improvement I have ever measured came from reading my own data loader properly.

  2. ii

    Verify that what you assume is learning, learned.

    Open the checkpoint. Look at the weights. A component can appear in every diagram you have ever drawn and still be inert, and nothing in the loss curve will tell you.

  3. iii

    Distrust your own good numbers first.

    A result that looks excellent is a hypothesis about your evaluation, not about your model. Several of the most impressive numbers I have produced turned out to be measuring something else.

  4. iv

    Score it the way a practitioner would.

    Continuous stream, not curated windows. Patient-level splits, not random ones. Events, not frames. Alarms per day, because that is the number that decides whether anyone keeps wearing the device.

  5. v

    Reach for the physics before the parameters.

    Phase-based connectivity because it is immune to volume conduction. Phase-space embedding because it bypasses fragile peak detection. If a component has no physical justification, I remove it rather than tune it.

  6. vi

    Map the ceiling instead of assuming it.

    Pull every lever, write down what each one measured, then go and find what a trained human reaches on the same signal. Sometimes the honest finding is that the limit is the hardware, and that is a result worth reporting.
